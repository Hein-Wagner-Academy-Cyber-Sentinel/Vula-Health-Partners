DATA GOVERNANCE & POPIA COMPLIANCE STATEMENT
Regulatory Posture & Role
The organisation operates in strict accordance with the **Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)**, functioning as a **Responsible Party** (for direct platform operations) and an authorized **Operator** (processing on behalf of partner healthcare institutions, hospitals, and clinics).

4 Core Compliance Architecture
Special Personal Information (Sections 26 & 32)**
Health data is classified as Special Personal Information. Processing is strictly restricted to medical practitioners, registered healthcare institutions, and partner facilities exclusively for clinical triage, diagnosis, and patient care management.
Accountability & Governance (Condition 1)**
A registered Information Officer** oversees regulatory engagement with the Information Regulator. Operational policies are formally codified within maintained **POPIA & PAIA Compliance Manuals.
Lawful Basis & Purpose Limitation (Conditions 2 & 3)**
Data collection is limited to minimal, necessary clinical parameters justified by clinical necessity, statutory mandates, or explicit consent. Clinical data retention strictly adheres to HPCSA guidelines (minimum 6-year retention for standard adult records).
Processing Boundaries & Data Quality (Conditions 4 & 5)**
Secondary commercialization, marketing, or external analytics on identifiable patient data are strictly prohibited; research applications require irreversible anonymization. Systematic verification controls ensure clinical records remain complete, accurate, and up to date.
Technical & Physical Safeguards (Condition 7)**
Architecture Zero Trust framework, multi-factor authentication (MFA), role-based access control (RBAC), and AES-256 / TLS 1.3 encryption at rest and in transit.
Operator Agreements (Section 21):** All third-party infrastructure and cloud service providers execute binding Data Processing Agreements upholding equivalent statutory safeguards.
Incident Response (Section 22):** Mandatory protocols are established to immediately notify the Information Regulator and affected data subjects upon detection of any confirmed or suspected compromise.
Cross-Border Transfers (Section 72)
Hosting and SaaS processing remain localized within South Africa or within compliant offshore jurisdictions providing substantially similar legal safeguards (e.g., GDPR-aligned regions), supported by binding corporate rules.
